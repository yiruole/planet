The Bonds of Id

Oil on canvas  ·  40 × 60 cm  ·  2025

The painting stages a contest between Id, Ego, and Superego. The sinuous blue-green torso at the center embodies the Id in its raw, irrational force—severed from its rational head, extending as pure instinct into darkness. The grey-white classical stone sculptures on either side stand for the rigid, repressive Superego and the reality-bound Ego. Fluorescent-pink bonds act as a Freudian repression mechanism, forcibly suturing instinct to civilized order, generating an agonized tearing sensation in the dark depths of the unconscious. Above, a red sun is consumed by unconscious darkness—the daylight of reason fading—as the ego undergoes its painful, inevitable alchemical transformation.

Mirror — Balance

Oil on canvas  ·  40 × 60 cm  ·  Nov. 2025

The water's surface and the bubbles at the chest form a dual mirror. Drawing on Lacan's Mirror Stage, the deep eye in the foreground and the blurred reflection beneath the water surface capture the subject's attempt—in the undifferentiated pre-linguistic stage—to confirm its own wholeness through an external image. Yet the mirrored self is always a fiction, a misrecognition: like the disembodied white hand rising from the void to cradle the face, a symbol of the Other that simultaneously constructs and disciplines the "I."

The work introduces the physical equilibrium between water and vapor. Water is the sunken, fluid unconscious; the rising bubbles and crystal spheres are the condensed, sublimated gaze of consciousness. Within this closed psychic field, evaporation and condensation achieve dynamic balance: the Id's unconscious desire ceaselessly probes outward, becoming alienated as rational self-identity, while self-reflection constantly precipitates back into the depths of the unconscious sea.

The Non-Existent Atom

Oil on canvas  ·  70 × 70 cm  ·  Nov. 2025

In this work, I attempt to approach existence itself from the side that has been declared "non-existent"—to explore the outermost boundary of scientific discourse from within its own terms.

For me, "non-existence" does not mean something is absent from the world. It means something has been deleted from the range of what a given theory or language permits to be named.

This work is a small attempt to sketch at that blind spot.

Mental Breakdown Caused by Uncertainty

Oil on canvas  ·  70 × 90 cm  ·  Nov. 2025

This work places quantum physics and psychoanalysis in direct confrontation. Wave-particle duality and the uncertainty principle reveal that reality is fundamentally unstable—it cannot be fully verified or fixed in place. Likewise, the human subject is produced through a fragile suture between the Imaginary and the Symbolic: on one side, how we see ourselves; on the other, how language, culture, and social order name and assign us. This construction makes identity possible while always carrying within it the possibility of its own collapse. Brain fog is a collective symptom.

Anxiety Cured My Existential Emptiness

Oil on canvas  ·  70 × 90 cm  ·  Dec. 2025

Using ironic visual rhetoric, this painting depicts a psyche willing to drink poison to quench its thirst—anything to escape the existential void. Existential emptiness is the vacuum the subject falls into when faced with the absence of the Other. To combat a nothingness more terrifying than death, the subject forces its way through chaotic sensory experience via symbolic nodes, suturing fragmented appearances into a precariously balanced self. The Tangut character in the upper right corner stands for the incompleteness and inherent meaninglessness of all symbolic systems.

Structure Exceeds Appearance

Oil on canvas  ·  80 × 100 cm  ·  Dec. 2025

This painting carries the feel of an experimental manuscript, a notebook of the psyche. It renders visually how the Symbolic Order disciplines and anchors the Imaginary. Psychological reality is determined not by surface appearances but by the invisible underlying structures of language, symbol, and the unconscious.

At the base of the canvas, heavy, struggling colors and half-glimpsed human faces represent raw, untamed libidinal drives. But what governs the entire composition is an unconscious coordinate grid built from rational terms—reconstruction, spatial power, performativity, instability—and radiating lines. The core at the center, locked down by red and black lines, is the Lacanian point de capiton: the anchoring node through which structure forcibly pierces chaotic sensory experience, suturing fragmented appearances into a precariously balanced subject. The Tangut character in the upper right corner stands for the incompleteness and inherent meaninglessness of all symbolic systems.

Threshold: The Residual Darkness
at the Edge of Jouissance

Oil on canvas  ·  80 × 100 cm  ·  Dec. 2025

This painting attempts to visualize Lacan's dialectic of jouissance and plus-de-jouir—surplus enjoyment. The "edge of ecstasy" in the title refers to a limit experience that exceeds the pleasure principle and approaches the boundary of pain. On the right, a female figure enveloped in blazing pink and blood-red clouds embodies a subject in the grip of total ecstatic experience, body and spirit fully ignited. Her face is distorted by light; her eyes receded into sealed voids—consciousness drowning in jouissance, reason retreating. Yet the ecstasy can never be pure. On the left, a cold blue undercurrent; on the water's surface, a solitary eye gazes without expression. The unnamed thing. A chrysalis waiting to rupture.

Untitled

Oil on canvas  ·  70 × 70 cm  ·  Feb. 2026

This painting had no blueprint, no plan made in advance. It was painted entirely by following the feeling of the moment. While working, the rational mind was set aside — I relied purely on intuition and bodily instinct, adding and subtracting on the canvas. In the end, a rough crack rose naturally at the center. It resists easy definition, yet it is visually arresting: at once like a fracture splitting open in hard mineral rock, like a galaxy igniting across a night sky, and like the primal cleft of a womb from which life emerges. It represents no lofty theory. In that moment, whatever felt most right to paint is simply what it became.

Overhead

The earth appears here as a transparent vessel filled with water.
